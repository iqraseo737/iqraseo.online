SEO

What is SEO and why does your business need it?

SEO helps customers find your website on Google without paying for every click.

Read articleClose article

SEO stands for search engine optimization. It is the work of improving your website so it appears higher on Google when people search for your product or service.

It has three main parts:

  • On-page SEO: titles, headings, keywords and content on your pages.
  • Technical SEO: speed, mobile friendliness and clean site structure.
  • Off-page SEO: backlinks from other trusted websites.

Unlike ads, SEO traffic does not stop when you stop paying. It takes time to build, but a well-ranked page can bring customers for years.

Off Page SEO

How to judge the quality of a backlink

Not every backlink helps. Some can even hurt your rankings. Here is what to check.

Read articleClose article

A backlink is a link from another website to yours. Google treats good links as a vote of trust. Before taking any link, check these points:

  • Relevance: is the website about your niche or a related one?
  • Real traffic: does the site get real visitors from Google?
  • Content quality: are the articles useful and well written?
  • Indexing: are the site's pages indexed on Google?
  • Link profile: does it link out to spam or unrelated sites?

A few links from relevant sites with real traffic are worth more than hundreds of links from weak, unrelated sites.

Ads

Google Ads or Meta Ads: which one is right for you?

Both work, but for different goals. The right choice depends on how your customers buy.

Read articleClose article

Google Ads shows your ad when someone is already searching for what you sell. It works best for services people actively look for, like a plumber, a lawyer or a clinic.

Meta Ads (Facebook and Instagram) shows your ad to people based on their interests, even if they are not searching. It works best for visual products, new brands and offers people did not know they wanted.

  • Need leads from people searching right now? Start with Google Ads.
  • Selling a visual product or building a brand? Start with Meta Ads.
  • Have the budget? Use both and retarget visitors across them.
On Page SEO

6 on-page SEO mistakes that hold your website back

Small mistakes on your pages can keep you off the first page. Most are easy to fix.

Read articleClose article
  • Missing or duplicate title tags. Every page needs its own clear title.
  • No meta description. It affects how many people click your result.
  • More than one H1. Use one main heading per page.
  • Keyword stuffing. Write for people first, not for search engines.
  • Heavy images. Large images slow the page. Compress them.
  • No internal links. Link your related pages to each other.

Fixing these basics is often the fastest way to improve rankings on an existing site.

Web Development

Why a fast, mobile-friendly website matters

Most visitors come from a phone. If your site is slow, they leave before it loads.

Read articleClose article

Your website is often the first impression of your business. A slow or broken mobile site loses customers and wastes the money you spend on ads and SEO.

  • Google uses the mobile version of your site for ranking.
  • Page speed is part of Google's ranking signals.
  • A clear layout and visible contact button bring more enquiries.

Test your site on your own phone. If it is hard to read, slow to open or hard to contact you from, that is the first thing to fix.

Content Writing

How to write content that ranks on Google

Good content answers the reader's question better than the pages already ranking.

Read articleClose article
  • Start with the search intent. What does the person really want to know?
  • Pick one main keyword and use it in the title, first paragraph and a heading.
  • Use clear headings so readers can scan the page.
  • Keep sentences short and avoid filler.
  • Add real value: examples, steps or answers competitors missed.
  • Update old articles so they stay accurate.

Content written for real readers tends to rank better and convert better than content written only for keywords.
